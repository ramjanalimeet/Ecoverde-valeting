A musty smell, white or green spots on the seats, a fogged-up windscreen that never quite clears — **mould in a car** is more common than most people think, especially after a damp British winter. It looks bad, smells worse and can be a real problem for anyone with allergies or asthma.

This guide explains why it happens, what you can safely do yourself and how professional mould removal works.

![Mould-covered car seats and dashboard before an eco steam clean](/images/mould-car-interior-before-cleaning.webp "A real mould job before we started.")

## Why does mould grow in cars?

Mould needs three things: moisture, warmth and something organic to feed on (dust, skin cells, food crumbs). Cars provide all three. Common causes include:

- Leaking door or window seals, sunroof drains or boot seals
- Blocked scuttle panel or air-conditioning drains
- Wet coats, umbrellas and gym kit left inside
- A car parked for weeks without being driven or aired
- Spilled drinks soaking into carpets and underlay

## Signs your car has a mould problem

| Sign | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Musty or damp smell | Moisture in carpets or the air-con system |
| White, grey or green spots | Surface mould on fabric, leather or plastics |
| Constant window misting | High humidity inside the cabin |
| Wet footwell carpets | A leak — find the source before cleaning |
| Allergy symptoms when driving | Spores in the cabin air |

## Can you remove car mould yourself?

For a small patch of surface mould you can:

1. Park in the sun and open all the doors to dry the cabin.
2. Vacuum the area thoroughly, ideally with a HEPA filter.
3. Clean with a mild, car-safe interior cleaner and a microfibre cloth.
4. Dry completely and leave the windows open if it is safe to do so.

But DIY rarely reaches the source. Mould lives in carpet underlay, seat foam and air vents, and household chemicals can damage leather, Alcantara and trim. If the mould covers more than a small area, or the smell returns, it needs a professional clean ([Clean Me](https://www.cleanme.co.uk/services/mobile-car-valeting/car-mould-removal/)).

> **Warning:** do not mix bleach with other cleaners, and do not use bleach on car interiors. It can discolour fabric and leather, and the fumes are harmful in a small space.

## How to stop mould coming back

- Fix leaks — door seals, sunroof drains and scuttle drains are common culprits
- Never leave wet clothes, towels or umbrellas in the car
- Run the air-con regularly, even in winter, to dry the air
- Use the recirculation setting sparingly
- Air the car on dry days and drive it regularly
- Book an [interior deep clean](/services/interior-deep-clean) once or twice a year

## Frequently asked questions

### Is mould in a car dangerous?
Mould spores can trigger allergies, asthma and irritation, especially in an enclosed cabin. It is best removed promptly and professionally if it covers more than a small patch.

### Will mould come back after cleaning?
Not if the source of the damp is fixed. Cleaning removes the mould; stopping the moisture stops it returning. We point out likely causes, such as blocked drains or failed seals.

### Does ozone kill mould?
Ozone is very effective at eliminating mould and mildew smells and reaching hidden areas, but it works best after a thorough steam clean and extraction, not instead of one.
