Handing back a lease car should be simple. But stained seats, kerbed alloys or a dirty boot can turn into charges on the final invoice. The good news: most end-of-lease charges are avoidable with a little preparation and a proper **end of lease car valet** a few days before collection.

This guide explains how fair wear and tear works, what the inspector looks at, and a simple timeline to follow.

> **Key takeaway:** plan your handback 8–12 weeks out, compare the car against the BVRLA Fair Wear and Tear guide, fix anything out of tolerance, and book a lease return valet 1–3 days before collection.

## What is "fair wear and tear"?

Leasing companies expect a car to show normal signs of use. The industry standard is the **BVRLA Fair Wear and Tear Guide**, which sets out what is acceptable when a leased vehicle is returned. You are not charged for normal wear — only for damage or condition beyond those standards ([BVRLA](https://www.bvrla.co.uk/resource/returning-your-leased-vehicle.html)).

Charges are not trivial: one Fleet News dataset put the average end-of-contract damage charge at around **£314** ([Fleet News](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/fleet-industry-news/2020/01/22/average-wear-and-tear-charges-fall-by-23)).

## Your lease return timeline

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 8–12 weeks before | Download the BVRLA guide and your lessor's return conditions. Book the collection date. |
| 6–8 weeks before | Walk round the car in daylight. Photograph any damage. Get quotes for out-of-tolerance repairs. |
| 3–4 weeks before | Complete repairs. Check service history, MOT, both keys and the locking wheel nut. |
| 1–3 days before | Book your [lease return valet](/services/lease-return-valet). |
| Collection day | Take dated photos and video of every panel, the interior and the mileage. Keep them. |

Timing guidance: [Leasing.com](https://leasing.com/guides/fair-wear-tear-guide/) and [Gateway2Lease](https://gateway2lease.com/fair-wear-and-tear).

## Common end-of-lease charges that are easy to avoid

- **Dirty interiors.** A car returned dirty can't be inspected properly and may attract a cleaning charge.
- **Smells.** Smoke and pet odours are hard to hide and expensive to remove at a lessor's prep centre.
- **Missing items.** Spare key, locking wheel nut, parcel shelf, charging cables and the owner's pack.
- **Service gaps.** Missed services or missing paperwork are a frequent cause of charges ([Fleet UK](https://fleetuk.com/end-of-contract-guide)).
- **Kerbed alloys.** Large alloy wheels are particularly vulnerable — a refurb is often cheaper than the charge ([Parkers](https://www.parkers.co.uk/pages/article/car/cararticle.aspx?id=160532)).

> **Tip:** take your own dated photos immediately after the valet. If a charge is disputed later, a clean, well-lit set of pictures is your best evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I book an end of lease valet?
Ideally 1–3 days before your handback, so the car is at its best on inspection day without time to get dirty again.

### Does a valet remove scratches?
A valet cleans and protects the paint, which can reduce the appearance of very light marks. Deeper scratches need [paint correction](/services/paint-correction) or a smart repair — we will tell you which is worth doing.

### Will a valet stop me being charged?
A valet removes the cleaning-related reasons for charges and lets the inspector see the true condition. Damage beyond fair wear and tear still needs repairing, so check the car against the BVRLA guide early.
