## How much water does a car wash use?

Water use varies enormously depending on how the car is washed. Published UK figures put the comparison like this:

| Wash method | Typical water use per car |
|---|---|
| Hand car wash | 100–300 litres |
| Automatic machine wash | 150–400 litres (some recycled) |
| Steam wash | 5–15 litres |
| Rinseless / waterless wash | Around 5–20 litres |

Source: [Get Car Clean — eco car wash options in the UK](https://www.getcarclean.com/blog/eco-car-wash-uk). Figures are typical ranges, not exact measurements.

At Ecoverde we use **low-water and waterless techniques, steam and biodegradable products**, so there is no foamy run-off going into Ealing's drains — and our electric van means no generator fumes either.

> **Good to know:** "eco-friendly" is not a regulated term. Ask what products a valeter uses and what happens to the waste water — not just how many litres they use.

## Why a hand wash is safer than a brush wash

Automatic brush washes and shared sponges drag grit across the clear coat, which is how most swirl marks are created. Our wash method is designed to avoid that:

1. **Snow foam pre-wash** softens and lifts dirt.
2. **Two-bucket method** — one bucket for shampoo, one to rinse the mitt — keeps grit off the paint.
3. **Soft wash mitts**, never brushes or sponges, on painted surfaces.
4. **Separate tools for wheels** so brake dust never touches the bodywork.
5. **Microfibre drying** for a spot-free finish.

If your paint is already swirled, ask about [paint correction](/services/paint-correction).
